# How to Clean and Sterilise a Menstrual Cup: A Simple Routine

**TL;DR:** Cleaning your menstrual cup is straightforward: rinse with cold water, wash with mild unscented soap, clear the suction holes, and rinse well. Between cycles, boil it for three to five minutes. Avoid bleach, vinegar, tea tree oil, hydrogen peroxide, antibacterial soaps and harsh rubbing alcohol. Always follow the instructions that come with your cup.

## What does daily cleaning involve?

The routine for looking after your cup during your period is simple. The daily routine is simple:

- Rinse the cup with cold water.
- Wash it with a mild, unscented, water-based soap.
- Clear the suction holes.
- Rinse away all soap residue.

That's it for day-to-day care. The cup can be used for up to eight hours before removing and cleaning it, so you'll typically clean it once or twice a day depending on your flow.

## How do you sterilise the cup between cycles?

The instructions give a boiling method for sterilising:

- Place the cup in a pot of boiling water for three to five minutes.
- Keep the cup away from the base of the pot.

That's the between-cycle step.

## What should you avoid when cleaning your cup?

Keep several things away from the cup:

- Bleach
- Vinegar
- Tea tree oil
- Hydrogen peroxide
- Antibacterial soaps
- Harsh rubbing alcohol

## Is a menstrual cup right for you?

A menstrual cup isn't for everyone. Some people find there's a learning curve with insertion and removal, and comfort can take a few cycles to get used to. If you experience pain, stop using it and speak to a qualified health professional.
