Most Australian households go through a bottle of laundry detergent every four to six weeks. That's another plastic bottle in the recycling (if it even gets there), and for a lot of people, another dose of fragrance and chemicals landing directly on skin and bedsheets, load after load.
If you've ever stood in the supermarket aisle wondering whether there's a simpler way to do laundry, you're not alone. It's one of the most common questions we hear from people starting a lower-waste routine: is there something that actually replaces detergent, or is it all marketing?
Short answer: yes, and it's called a reusable laundry ball.
## The real problem: laundry is a bigger waste habit than people realise  
Detergent bottles are one of those "invisible" waste sources. Nobody thinks twice about tossing one in the bin or the recycling, until you add it up. A household doing a few loads a week can go through 10 to 13 plastic bottles a year. Multiply that across a suburb, a city, a country, and it's a genuinely large amount of single-use plastic for something as routine as clean clothes.
There's a second layer to this problem too: what's actually in the bottle. Many mainstream detergents rely on added fragrance and synthetic additives to make clothes "smell clean." For households managing sensitive skin, eczema, or fragrance sensitivities, that's not a small thing, it's the difference between a comfortable load of washing and an itchy one.
## How a reusable laundry ball solves both problems at once
